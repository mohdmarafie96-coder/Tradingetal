# 07.4 — Fundamentals and the Calendar

## What moves markets

Technical analysis describes what price has done. Fundamental analysis addresses why, and
for a short-term trader its main practical value is **knowing when volatility is
scheduled** rather than forecasting direction.

## Currency drivers

**Interest rates and central bank policy.** The dominant driver. Higher rates generally
attract capital and strengthen a currency. What matters is not the current rate but the
**expected change** relative to what is already priced in. A rate rise that markets fully
anticipated produces little movement; an unexpected hold can produce a great deal.

This is the single most important concept in trading news: **markets price expectations,
and react to surprises.** A strong number that is weaker than expected is bearish.

Key central banks: Federal Reserve (USD), European Central Bank (EUR), Bank of England
(GBP), Bank of Japan (JPY), Swiss National Bank (CHF), Reserve Bank of Australia (AUD),
Bank of Canada (CAD).

**Inflation.** CPI drives rate expectations and is therefore among the highest-impact
releases. US CPI regularly moves every major pair.

**Employment.** US non-farm payrolls, released the first Friday of each month, is the
single most volatile scheduled event in FX.

**Growth.** GDP, PMI surveys, retail sales, industrial production.

**Risk sentiment.** In risk-off conditions, capital moves to USD, JPY, CHF and gold.
This is why apparently unrelated instruments move together, as Module 06.4 described.

**Politics.** Elections, referendums, trade policy, conflict. Unpredictable in both timing
and direction, and the main source of weekend gap risk.

## Index and share drivers

**Indices:** interest rates (they discount future earnings), aggregate corporate earnings,
economic growth, and risk sentiment.

**Shares:** earnings and guidance above all, plus sector conditions, management changes,
regulation, litigation and M&A. **Earnings dates are published in advance and are the
largest single gap risk a retail trader faces.**

## Commodity drivers

**Oil:** OPEC+ production decisions, US inventory data (EIA, Wednesdays), geopolitics,
demand expectations.

**Gold:** real interest rates (the dominant driver), the dollar, risk sentiment, central
bank buying.

**Agricultural:** weather, harvest reports, export policy.

## The economic calendar

A schedule of upcoming releases with expected values, previous values, and an impact
rating. Free versions are published by Forex Factory, Investing.com, DailyFX and most
brokers.

Each entry gives:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Time | Release time, in a timezone you must check |
| Currency | Which currency it primarily affects |
| Impact | High, medium, low — a rough volatility expectation |
| Actual | The released figure |
| Forecast | The consensus expectation |
| Previous | Last period's figure |

**The surprise is what matters: actual versus forecast, not actual versus previous.**

### How to use it

**Check it before every trading session.** This takes two minutes and prevents a whole
category of avoidable loss.

**The minimum discipline:**

1. Note every high-impact event for your instrument's currencies in the next 24 hours
2. Do not open new positions in the 30 minutes before a high-impact release
3. Decide in advance whether existing positions stay open through it
4. Expect wider spreads and worse fills for 15 to 30 minutes afterwards

**Trading the news directly is not a beginner strategy.** The reasons are structural:
spreads widen dramatically, slippage is severe, the initial move frequently reverses within
minutes, and you are competing with participants who receive and process the data faster
than your platform can display it. The apparent opportunity is real; your ability to
capture it is not.

### Typical high-impact events

| Event | Frequency | Affects |
|---|---|---|
| Central bank rate decisions | ~8 per year each | That currency, strongly |
| US non-farm payrolls | Monthly, first Friday | Everything |
| CPI releases | Monthly | That currency, strongly |
| GDP | Quarterly | That currency |
| PMI surveys | Monthly | Moderate |
| Central bank speeches | Frequent | Variable, sometimes large |
| US crude inventories | Weekly, Wednesday | Oil |

## Sentiment and positioning

**COT reports.** The US Commodity Futures Trading Commission publishes weekly Commitments
of Traders data showing positioning by category in futures markets. Extreme positioning is
sometimes read as a contrarian signal. It is delayed by several days and covers futures
rather than spot, so treat it as slow background context.

**Broker sentiment data.** Some brokers publish the percentage of their clients long
versus short. Given that most retail clients lose money, extreme retail positioning is
sometimes used as a contrarian indicator. Treat with caution: the sample is one broker's
clients, and the effect is weak.

**Volatility indices.** VIX and similar measure expected volatility in equity markets. High
VIX indicates stress. A useful regime indicator rather than a trading signal.

## How much fundamental analysis do you need?

For a short-term technical trader, less than you might think, but not zero:

**Essential:**
- Check the calendar before every session
- Know when your instrument's central bank meets
- Know earnings dates for any share you hold
- Understand the broad current regime — are rates rising or falling, is sentiment risk-on
  or risk-off?

**Useful:**
- Understand what drives your chosen instrument
- Follow the main scheduled releases for its currencies

**Not necessary:**
- Forecasting economic data
- Building macroeconomic models
- Trading releases directly

The realistic goal is **not being surprised**. You are not trying to out-forecast
economists. You are trying to avoid holding a leveraged position into an event you did not
know was scheduled.

## A worked scenario

You are long EUR/USD on an H4 technical setup, target three days out.

**Calendar check:**

| Day | Event |
|---|---|
| Tomorrow 13:30 UTC | US CPI (high impact) |
| Day 3, 12:45 UTC | ECB rate decision (high impact) |
| Day 3, 13:30 UTC | ECB press conference (high impact) |

Two high-impact events before your target. Options:

1. **Close before CPI**, re-enter after. Costs an extra spread, removes the risk.
2. **Reduce size by half** before CPI. Keeps exposure, halves the event risk.
3. **Hold full size.** Accept that a surprise could move price well beyond your stop with
   slippage.
4. **Tighten the stop.** Usually the worst option: a tight stop into a volatile release is
   almost certain to be triggered on the initial spike, often in the wrong direction,
   before price settles.

There is no single correct answer, and the choice depends on your plan. What is not
acceptable is discovering the ECB meeting after the fact. **The decision must be made in
advance and written in your plan** so that it is a rule rather than a reaction.

## Key points

- Markets price expectations and react to surprises: actual versus forecast is what matters
- Interest rate expectations are the dominant currency driver
- Earnings dates are the largest gap risk available to a retail trader
- Check the economic calendar before every session; it takes two minutes
- Do not open positions in the 30 minutes before a high-impact release
- Trading news directly is not a beginner strategy
- The goal of fundamental awareness is not being surprised, not forecasting
