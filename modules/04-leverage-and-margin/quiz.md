# Module 04 Quiz

Closed book. Calculator permitted. Tick one answer per question unless the question says
otherwise.

[Answers](../../reference/quiz-answers.md#module-04) unlock once you have submitted the
quiz.

**1.** Leverage and margin percentage are related how, and what margin percentage
corresponds to 20:1?

- [ ] a) Margin % = leverage × 2, so 20:1 is 40%
- [ ] b) They are unrelated
- [ ] c) Margin % = leverage / 100, so 20:1 is 20%
- [ ] d) Margin % = 1 / leverage, so 20:1 is 5%

**2.** At 30:1, what percentage adverse move eliminates the margin posted on a fully
leveraged position?

- [ ] a) 30%
- [ ] b) 10%
- [ ] c) 3.33%
- [ ] d) 0.33%

**3.** Two traders hold identical 0.2-lot positions with identical 30-pip stops. One has a
30:1 account, the other 10:1. Who loses more if stopped out?

- [ ] a) Neither — the loss is identical
- [ ] b) The 30:1 trader, because higher leverage means higher risk
- [ ] c) It depends on the instrument
- [ ] d) The 10:1 trader, because more capital is tied up

**4.** Which set of definitions is correct?

- [ ] a) Equity = balance − used margin; margin level = free margin × 100%
- [ ] b) Equity = balance + unrealised P&L; free margin = equity − used margin; margin level = (equity / used margin) × 100%
- [ ] c) Equity = balance; free margin = balance − used margin; margin level = balance / equity
- [ ] d) Equity = used margin + free margin; margin level = (used margin / equity) × 100%

**5.** Why is balance a misleading number to watch while positions are open?

- [ ] a) It is quoted in the instrument's currency rather than your account's
- [ ] b) It includes used margin, which double-counts the capital at work
- [ ] c) It is realised cash, so it does not move while equity falls on open positions
- [ ] d) It updates only once a day, at the platform's rollover time

**6.** At what margin level must a regulated broker begin closing retail positions, and
what is that level measured against?

- [ ] a) 25%, measured against notional exposure
- [ ] b) 50%, measured against your account balance
- [ ] c) 100%, measured against required initial margin
- [ ] d) 50%, measured against required initial margin

**7.** $6,000 account, long 1.2 lots EUR/USD at 1.0900 at 30:1. Calculate used margin and
margin level.

- [ ] a) $4,360 and 137.6%
- [ ] b) $13,080 and 45.9%
- [ ] c) $4,360 and 72.7%
- [ ] d) $3,633 and 165.2%

**8.** For the position in the previous question, approximately how many pips of adverse
movement trigger the 50% close-out, and what is the loss as a percentage of the account?

- [ ] a) About 500 pips, a 100% loss
- [ ] b) About 324 pips, a 64.7% loss
- [ ] c) About 250 pips, a 50% loss
- [ ] d) About 141 pips, a 28% loss

**9.** What is effective leverage, and what is a reasonable maximum for a learning trader?

- [ ] a) Used margin / equity; keep it under 50%
- [ ] b) The leverage setting on the account; keep it under 30:1
- [ ] c) Total notional of open positions / equity; keep it under 5:1
- [ ] d) Equity / notional; keep it above 2:1

**10.** Why does a stop loss not guarantee your exit price?

- [ ] a) It does guarantee the price; that is the whole purpose of the order
- [ ] b) Stops are checked only once a minute, so fast moves are missed entirely
- [ ] c) Brokers deliberately fill stops at prices worse than the level you set
- [ ] d) It triggers at your level and becomes a market order, so a gap fills you lower

**11.** What is negative balance protection, and what is the one thing it does NOT protect
you from?

- [ ] a) You cannot lose more than your account balance; it does not cap loss at your stop
- [ ] b) Your loss is capped at your stop level; it does not cover commission
- [ ] c) Your deposit is guaranteed; it does not cover overnight financing
- [ ] d) Losses beyond 50% are refunded; it does not apply to FX positions

**12.** You are long a share CFD with a stop 5% below entry. The company reports earnings
tonight. Select the two actions that address the risk. (select all that apply)

- [ ] a) Close or substantially reduce the position before the announcement
- [ ] b) Use a guaranteed stop loss, honoured at your price regardless of the gap
- [ ] c) Tighten the stop to 2% so less is at risk
- [ ] d) Add to the position to average down if it gaps
