# Module 02 — What a CFD Actually Is

**Time:** ~4 hours · **Prerequisites:** Modules 00–01

The contract itself. What you are agreeing to, with whom, what you do and do not own,
how going short works, and what the regulator has decided you may and may not do.

Most people who trade CFDs cannot accurately describe the contract they have entered.
That is a poor position from which to manage risk.

## Lessons

1. [The contract](01-the-contract.md) — what a CFD is, precisely
2. [CFDs versus owning the asset](02-vs-owning.md) — a direct comparison
3. [Going long and going short](03-long-and-short.md) — both directions, with arithmetic
4. [Regulation and jurisdiction](04-regulation.md) — what protects you, and where

## Learning outcomes

By the end of this module you can:

- Define a CFD precisely, including who the counterparty is
- List what you own, and what rights you have, when holding a CFD
- Explain why shorting a CFD is mechanically symmetrical to going long, and why the risk
  is not
- State the leverage caps, margin close-out rule and negative balance protection under
  UK/EU and Australian retail rules
- Explain what "professional client" status changes and why accepting it is usually a bad
  idea
