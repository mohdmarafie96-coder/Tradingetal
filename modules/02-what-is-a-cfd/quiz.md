**1.** Which sentence defines a CFD correctly, including the counterparty?

- [ ] a) A leveraged purchase of the underlying shares, held in custody by your broker
- [ ] b) An exchange-traded contract to buy the underlying asset at a fixed future date
- [ ] c) A peer-to-peer contract matched against another retail trader's position
- [ ] d) A cash-settled agreement with your broker to exchange the price difference between open and close

**2.** You hold 1,000 share CFDs in a company that goes into administration. What is your
claim on the company's assets?

- [ ] a) None — you own no shares; your contract is with the broker
- [ ] b) A claim limited to the margin you posted
- [ ] c) A preferential claim, because CFDs are collateralised
- [ ] d) The same claim as any other shareholder

**3.** Why do CFDs incur overnight financing when outright shares do not?

- [ ] a) Because dividends on the underlying must be replicated synthetically each night
- [ ] b) Because the broker funds exposure far larger than your margin, and charges nightly
- [ ] c) Because regulators require brokers to levy a nightly charge on leveraged risk
- [ ] d) Because CFDs expire monthly and the position must be rolled to the next contract

**4.** Under UK/EU retail rules, what leverage is available on a major FX pair, and what
margin percentage does that imply?

- [ ] a) 20:1, implying 5% margin
- [ ] b) 10:1, implying 10% margin
- [ ] c) 30:1, implying 3.33% margin
- [ ] d) 50:1, implying 2% margin

**5.** At what level of equity relative to required margin must a broker begin closing
your positions?

- [ ] a) 100%
- [ ] b) 75%
- [ ] c) 50%
- [ ] d) 25%

**6.** Which historical event most clearly demonstrated why negative balance protection is
needed?

- [ ] a) The collapse of Lehman Brothers in September 2008
- [ ] b) The flash crash in US equities in May 2010
- [ ] c) The negative WTI crude oil settlement in April 2020
- [ ] d) The Swiss National Bank removing the EUR/CHF 1.20 floor in January 2015

**7.** A broker offers you professional client status with higher leverage. Select every
protection you would lose. (select all that apply)

- [ ] a) The ability to place stop loss orders
- [ ] b) Negative balance protection
- [ ] c) Segregation of client money
- [ ] d) Retail leverage caps
- [ ] e) Compensation scheme eligibility

**8.** You are short 200 share CFDs over an ex-dividend date. The gross dividend is $0.80
and the net is $0.68. What is the adjustment to your account?

- [ ] a) $160 debited
- [ ] b) $136 debited
- [ ] c) $136 credited
- [ ] d) $160 credited

**9.** Long positions have bounded maximum loss and short positions do not. What
practically limits the short loss in a regulated retail account?

- [ ] a) A regulatory cap of twice the margin posted
- [ ] b) The 50% margin close-out and negative balance protection, which cap the loss at your account balance
- [ ] c) The broker's obligation to buy back the position at your entry price
- [ ] d) Nothing — the loss really is unlimited in practice

**10.** A firm advertises a 100% deposit bonus and 400:1 leverage. What does this tell
you, and how do clients usually discover the problem?

- [ ] a) The firm is newly licensed; the problem is usually slow support
- [ ] b) It is a competitive regulated offer; the problem is usually high spreads
- [ ] c) The firm is outside UK/EU/AU regimes; clients discover a trading-volume requirement blocking withdrawal
- [ ] d) Nothing unusual; bonuses are standard across the industry
