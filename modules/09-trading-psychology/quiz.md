# Module 09 Quiz

Closed book. Tick one answer per question unless the question says otherwise.

[Answers](../../reference/quiz-answers.md#module-09) unlock once you have submitted the
quiz.

**1.** Define the disposition effect and explain why it is fatal to expectancy.

- [ ] a) Trading too often; it is fatal because costs accumulate quickly
- [ ] b) Sizing up after a win; it is fatal because variance rises sharply
- [ ] c) Preferring familiar instruments; it is fatal because it concentrates
- [ ] d) Selling winners early and holding losers; it inverts your expectancy

**2.** Why is outcome bias particularly dangerous in trading, given that markets are
noisy?

- [ ] a) Because noise lets bad process produce good short-term outcomes
- [ ] b) Because it causes traders to over-journal and lose focus
- [ ] c) Because outcomes in markets are unknowable in principle
- [ ] d) Because it makes traders far too cautious after a run of losses

**3.** In the tilt spiral, at which step is intervention realistically possible, and why
not later?

- [ ] a) At the first loss, because that is when the discomfort begins
- [ ] b) At the recovery urge, before size and emotion make stopping unlikely
- [ ] c) At the escalation step, because the damage becomes visible there
- [ ] d) After the large loss, during the post-mortem that follows it

**4.** You feel reluctant to take a setup that meets all your criteria. How do you
distinguish fear from prudence, and what does fear usually indicate?

- [ ] a) They cannot be told apart; treat all hesitation as a reason to skip
- [ ] b) Fear indicates that the market is about to reverse against you
- [ ] c) Prudence names a rule; diffuse fear usually means the size is too large
- [ ] d) Fear is specific and prudence vague; fear means the setup is invalid

**5.** Why is boredom a significant risk factor, and what is the structural response?

- [ ] a) It reduces concentration; the response is caffeine and shorter sessions
- [ ] b) Boredom is not a risk factor; it has no bearing on trade selection
- [ ] c) It causes traders to miss valid setups; the response is more screen time
- [ ] d) Empty screen time generates unplanned trades; do not sit at the platform

**6.** "I need to make that money back." What is precisely wrong with this as a goal?

- [ ] a) The market has no memory of your loss and owes you no recovery
- [ ] b) Nothing; it is a reasonable short-term objective to hold
- [ ] c) It ignores the tax implications of realising the loss
- [ ] d) It is far too vague a goal to act on in any specific way

**7.** Select the examples that correctly replace a willpower-based control with a
structural one. (select all that apply)

- [ ] a) Bracket orders placed at entry, instead of "I won't move my stop"
- [ ] b) A broker-level daily loss limit with a cooling-off period, instead of "I'll stop after losses"
- [ ] c) A printed sizing table calculated before the session, instead of "I'll stick to my size"
- [ ] d) Reminding yourself each morning to be disciplined
- [ ] e) Platform closed outside defined review times, instead of "I won't overtrade"

**8.** Select the conditions under which you should stop trading entirely. (select all
that apply)

- [ ] a) You are trading to change how you feel
- [ ] b) You hit your maximum drawdown limit
- [ ] c) Plan compliance stays below 80% despite structural countermeasures
- [ ] d) It is affecting your sleep, work, relationships or health
- [ ] e) You have had three losing trades in a row
