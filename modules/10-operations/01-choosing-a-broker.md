# 10.1 — Choosing a Broker

## The order of priorities

Most comparison sites rank brokers by spread. That is roughly the fourth most important
consideration.

The correct order:

1. **Regulation and safety of funds** — can you get your money back?
2. **Total cost** — spread plus commission plus financing plus conversion
3. **Execution quality** — do you get the price you see?
4. **Instrument range and contract sizes** — can you size correctly?
5. **Platform and tools**
6. **Support and withdrawal process**

A broker with the tightest spreads and no meaningful regulation is not cheap. It is a risk
of total loss with a small discount attached.

## 1. Regulation

**Verify the specific legal entity on your account agreement**, on the regulator's own
public register. Not the brand. Not a badge on the website.

| Regulator | Jurisdiction | Register |
|---|---|---|
| FCA | UK | https://register.fca.org.uk |
| BaFin | Germany | https://www.bafin.de |
| CySEC | Cyprus | https://www.cysec.gov.cy |
| AMF | France | https://www.amf-france.org |
| ASIC | Australia | https://asic.gov.au |
| MAS | Singapore | https://eservices.mas.gov.sg/fid |
| FSCA | South Africa | https://www.fsca.co.za |
| FINMA | Switzerland | https://www.finma.ch |

**Check for entity switching.** A very common structure: a brand operates "Broker Ltd"
regulated by the FCA and "Broker International Ltd" registered in St Vincent and the
Grenadines. Clients in some countries are onboarded to the offshore entity. The website,
the platform and the support staff are identical. The protections are not.

The entity name is in your client agreement. Read it before depositing.

**What good regulation gives you:**

- Leverage caps appropriate to the instrument
- Mandatory negative balance protection
- Segregated client money
- Compensation scheme membership
- An ombudsman or equivalent complaints route
- Mandated risk disclosures including the firm's own loss statistics

**Checklist:**

- [ ] Entity name from the client agreement located on the regulator's register
- [ ] Licence current, with permissions covering CFDs
- [ ] Disciplinary history checked
- [ ] Client money segregation confirmed in the terms
- [ ] Compensation scheme and limit identified
- [ ] Negative balance protection confirmed in writing
- [ ] Published percentage of losing retail accounts located

## 2. Total cost

Compare on **total cost for your actual trading pattern**, not on advertised spread.

Build this table for each candidate, using your real expected size and holding period:

| | Broker A | Broker B |
|---|---|---|
| Average spread on your instrument | | |
| Commission per lot per side | | |
| Spread cost per round trip at your size | | |
| Commission per round trip at your size | | |
| Overnight financing, long, per night | | |
| Overnight financing, short, per night | | |
| Currency conversion markup | | |
| Inactivity fee and trigger period | | |
| Withdrawal fee | | |
| **Total cost per round trip, held N nights** | | |
| **Estimated annual cost at your trade frequency** | | |

**Measure the spread yourself.** Advertised figures are averages taken during the most
liquid hours, or "from" figures that occur rarely. Open a demo and record the spread at the
times you actually trade, for a week.

## 3. Execution quality

Hard to assess before opening an account, and important.

**Indicators to look for:**
- Published execution statistics (some regulated brokers must publish these)
- Whether positive slippage is passed on, stated in the execution policy
- Minimum stop distances — smaller is better
- Independent reviews specifically mentioning execution during news

**Measure it after opening**, using the twenty-trade method from Module 05.3.

## 4. Instruments and contract sizes

- Does it offer the instruments you want?
- **What is the minimum trade size?** Critical on a small account, per Module 06.1. A
  broker with 0.01 lot minimums is materially more useful to a £500 account than one with
  0.1 lot minimums.
- Are contract specifications published clearly and in full?
- Trading hours for your instruments

## 5. Platform

MetaTrader 4 and 5, cTrader, TradingView integration, or proprietary. All are adequate.
The platform matters least of the six criteria and is marketed the most.

What actually matters: reliable order placement, visible contract specifications, a usable
order history export for your journal, and stability during volatile conditions.

## 6. Support and withdrawals

**Withdrawals are the single best test of a broker.** Before depositing anything
substantial:

1. Deposit a small amount
2. Trade a little
3. **Withdraw it**
4. Record how long it took and what was required

A broker that makes deposits instant and withdrawals difficult is telling you something
important, and it is the most common complaint pattern for problematic firms.

Search specifically for withdrawal complaints about any broker you are considering. The
pattern is visible in advance and people describe it in detail.

## Red flags

- Regulated only offshore, or not at all
- Leverage above jurisdictional caps for retail clients
- Deposit bonuses or trading incentives
- Pressure to deposit more, or to upgrade to professional status
- An "account manager" suggesting trades or offering to trade for you
- Guaranteed returns or profit claims of any kind
- Withdrawal difficulties reported by multiple independent users
- No published contract specifications
- No published loss statistics where they are legally required
- Cold calling

Any one of these is a reason to look elsewhere. There are many regulated brokers; there is
no need to accept a firm with warning signs.

## Key points

- Regulation first, cost second, execution third; spread alone is a poor comparison metric
- Verify the legal entity on the client agreement, on the regulator's own register
- Entity switching between onshore and offshore arms is common and consequential
- Compare total cost using your real size and holding period
- Minimum trade size determines whether correct position sizing is possible
- Test withdrawals early with a small amount
- Bonuses, guaranteed returns and account managers suggesting trades are disqualifying

## Exercise 10.1

**(a)** Take your current demo broker. Find the legal entity on the client agreement,
verify it on the regulator's register, and record licence number, permissions and any
disciplinary history.

**(b)** Build the total cost comparison table for three regulated brokers, using your real
intended size and holding period. Which is cheapest for your pattern? Is it the one with
the tightest advertised spread?

**(c)** For each of three brokers, record the minimum trade size on your instrument.
Calculate the minimum risk each implies with your typical stop distance, on your intended
account size. Which are usable?

**(d)** Find each broker's published percentage of losing retail accounts. Record them.
Note how prominently each is displayed.

**(e)** Read one broker's order execution policy in full. Summarise in five bullet points:
execution model, slippage treatment, minimum distances, close-only conditions, and how
prices are derived.
