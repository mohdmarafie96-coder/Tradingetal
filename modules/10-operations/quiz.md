# Module 10 Quiz

Closed book. Tick one answer per question unless the question says otherwise.

[Answers](../../reference/quiz-answers.md#module-10) unlock once you have submitted the
quiz.

**1.** Rank these in order of importance when choosing a broker: platform features, total
cost, regulation, spread.

- [ ] a) Spread, total cost, regulation, platform features
- [ ] b) Regulation, total cost, spread, platform features
- [ ] c) Total cost, spread, platform features, regulation
- [ ] d) Platform features, spread, total cost, regulation

**2.** What is entity switching, and why does it matter for the protections you receive?

- [ ] a) Migrating clients between two versions of the trading platform
- [ ] b) A broker transferring your open positions to a liquidity provider
- [ ] c) A brand running an onshore and an offshore entity, onboarding to either
- [ ] d) Changing your own account type between retail and professional

**3.** Comparing brokers by advertised spread is misleading. Select the other costs that
must be included. (select all that apply)

- [ ] a) Inactivity and withdrawal fees
- [ ] b) Overnight financing
- [ ] c) Commission
- [ ] d) The broker's own profit margin, which must be disclosed separately
- [ ] e) Currency conversion markup

**4.** What is the best practical test of a broker, and how do you perform it cheaply?

- [ ] a) Platform uptime — monitor the platform for a full month
- [ ] b) Support response time — send a test query and time the reply
- [ ] c) Spread measurement — record the quoted spread on a demo for a week
- [ ] d) Withdrawal — deposit a small amount, trade a little, then withdraw it

**5.** Select the items you should extract from a contract specification before trading a
new instrument. (select all that apply)

- [ ] a) Minimum and step size
- [ ] b) Margin requirement
- [ ] c) Contract size
- [ ] d) Trading hours and minimum stop distance
- [ ] e) The instrument's historical win rate
- [ ] f) Swap long and short, and the triple swap day

**6.** Why should one-click trading be disabled?

- [ ] a) It removes the confirmation step that catches order-entry errors
- [ ] b) It is not supported on any of the mobile trading platforms
- [ ] c) It voids your negative balance protection with the broker
- [ ] d) It executes at worse prices than the confirmation dialog does

**7.** When reconciling your journal against your platform statement, what is the most
common source of discrepancy?

- [ ] a) Timezone differences between the platform and your journal
- [ ] b) Overnight financing — it posts as separate account entries
- [ ] c) Slippage on entries that you recorded at the intended price
- [ ] d) Rounding differences on the position sizes you recorded

**8.** A firm offers a 50% deposit bonus. What does this tell you about its regulatory
status, and what is the usual catch?

- [ ] a) A promotion from a well-capitalised regulated firm; the catch is the spread
- [ ] b) Nothing in particular; the catch is usually a 30-day lock-in on funds
- [ ] c) Bonuses are banned in the UK, EU and Australia, so the firm is outside them
- [ ] d) It signals a newly issued licence; the catch is a limited instrument range

**9.** What is the economic argument against buying trading signals?

- [ ] a) Selling trading signals is illegal in most major jurisdictions
- [ ] b) Subscription costs exceed the size of a typical retail account
- [ ] c) The signals arrive too late for a retail trader to act on them
- [ ] d) If they worked, trading them would pay far more than selling them

**10.** Someone you met online introduces you to a trading platform showing strong
returns, and a small withdrawal succeeds. What is happening, and what should you do?

- [ ] a) This is the standard pattern of investment fraud; stop sending money
- [ ] b) The platform is legitimate; the successful withdrawal proves it
- [ ] c) Probably an unregulated but honest offshore broker; ask for a licence
- [ ] d) Wait and test with a second, larger withdrawal before deciding
