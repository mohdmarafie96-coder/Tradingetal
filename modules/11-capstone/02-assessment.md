## Part E — Judgement (Modules 02, 08, 09, 10)

**E1.** Which sentence defines a CFD correctly, naming the counterparty and stating what
you own?

- [ ] a) A margin loan against shares held for you; you own the shares outright
- [ ] b) A cash-settled agreement with your broker; you own nothing underlying
- [ ] c) An exchange-cleared derivative; you hold a claim on the clearing house
- [ ] d) A bilateral contract with another retail client; you own half of it

**E2.** A broker offers you professional client status with 100:1 leverage. What is your
answer, and what would you lose?

- [ ] a) Yes — the higher leverage lets you commit less margin per position
- [ ] b) Yes, provided you keep your effective leverage under five to one
- [ ] c) No — you would lose negative balance protection and leverage caps
- [ ] d) No — but only because the paperwork involved is onerous

**E3.** Your backtest shows +1.8R expectancy with an 85% win rate over 120 trades. What is
the most likely explanation, and what do you do?

- [ ] a) Reduce the profit target so the result looks more realistic
- [ ] b) You have found a strong edge; fund it at your full intended size
- [ ] c) The sample is too small; extend it to 300 trades and then fund
- [ ] d) You have made an error; find it before funding anything at all

**E4.** Your forward test shows positive expectancy but 74% plan compliance. What do you
conclude, and what is the correct next step?

- [ ] a) The result is uninterpretable; fix the execution, then retest
- [ ] b) Loosen the rules until compliance rises to an acceptable level
- [ ] c) Compliance does not matter while expectancy stays positive
- [ ] d) The strategy works; go live at the minimum position size

**E5.** You have taken four losses today, all within your rules. You notice a fifth setup.
Your plan's daily limit is three losses. What do you do?

- [ ] a) Take it only if the reward-to-risk on the setup exceeds three
- [ ] b) Stop. The plan says three and asking is itself the warning sign
- [ ] c) Take it at half size, since the setup itself is entirely valid
- [ ] d) Take it — the limit is a guideline and the losses followed rules

**E6.** A firm regulated in St Vincent and the Grenadines offers 400:1 leverage, a 100%
deposit bonus, and an account manager who will suggest trades. Select every red flag.
(select all that apply)

- [ ] a) A deposit bonus — prohibited under every major retail regime and usually carrying a volume requirement that locks your balance
- [ ] b) 400:1 leverage — a 0.25% move eliminates your margin
- [ ] c) Offshore-only regulation — no leverage caps, no negative balance protection, no compensation scheme, no effective recourse
- [ ] d) An account manager suggesting trades — a salesperson compensated on your activity, not an adviser acting for you

**E7.** Why is a guaranteed 8% monthly return not credible?

- [ ] a) Because tax on the returns reduces the net figure below 8%
- [ ] b) Because no regulator permits advertising a monthly return figure
- [ ] c) Because it compounds to about 152% a year, which nobody sustains
- [ ] d) Because 8% a month is only reachable with leverage above 100:1

**E8.** What is the single most important number in this course, and what is its
significance?

- [ ] a) The 1% risk-per-trade limit, which determines every position size
- [ ] b) The 50% margin close-out level, which defines when you are forced out
- [ ] c) The 30:1 retail leverage cap, which constrains every position taken
- [ ] d) That 70–85% of retail CFD accounts lose money: losing is the default
