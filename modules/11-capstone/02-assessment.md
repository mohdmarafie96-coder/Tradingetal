# 11.2 — Final Assessment

Closed book. Calculator permitted. Assume a USD account unless stated otherwise.

Answers and full workings: [quiz answers](../../reference/quiz-answers.md#final-assessment).

**Pass mark: 80%.** Below that, revisit the modules indicated against the questions you
missed. This assessment exists so that you find the gaps now rather than with money at
risk.

---

## Part A — Contract mechanics (Modules 03)

**A1.** You buy 0.45 lots of GBP/USD at 1.2680. Calculate units and notional value.

**A2.** Calculate the pip value in USD of 0.7 lots of USD/JPY at 151.40.

**A3.** Calculate the pip value in USD of 0.3 lots of EUR/GBP, with GBP/USD at 1.2650.

**A4.** You go short 0.6 lots of EUR/USD at a bid of 1.09240 and close at an ask of
1.08810. Commission is $3.50 per lot per side. Swap is +$1.10 per 0.1 lot per night, held
4 nights. Calculate gross P&L, total costs and net P&L.

**A5.** Long 5 CFDs of US 500 at 5,240. Benchmark rate 4.5%, broker markup 2.5%, 365-day
count. Calculate one night's financing and the cost of holding for 30 nights.

**A6.** You open a position on Tuesday and close it the following Tuesday. Your broker
applies triple swap on Wednesday. How many days of financing are charged?

---

## Part B — Leverage and margin (Module 04)

**B1.** State the margin requirement, in dollars, for 0.9 lots of EUR/USD at 1.0920 at
30:1 leverage.

**B2.** Your account equity is $9,000 and you hold positions with total notional of
$78,000. State your effective leverage and classify it.

**B3.** $7,000 account, long 1.4 lots of EUR/USD at 1.0900 at 30:1. Calculate used margin
and margin level.

**B4.** For the position in B3, calculate the approximate pip move that triggers a 50%
close-out, and the loss at that point as a percentage of the account.

**B5.** Explain why the trader in B3 loses substantially more than 50% of the account
despite the close-out being described as a "50%" rule.

**B6.** You are long 0.5 lots of GBP/USD at 1.2700 with a stop at 1.2640. The market
reopens on Sunday at 1.2490. Calculate intended loss, actual loss, and the multiple of
intended risk.

---

## Part C — Risk management (Module 06)

**C1.** $8,500 account, 0.75% risk. EUR/USD entry 1.0910, stop 1.0862. Calculate the
position size in lots, rounded down, and the actual risk in dollars.

**C2.** $15,000 account, 1% risk. Share CFD entry $73.40, stop $70.10. Calculate the number
of CFDs and the margin required at 20%.

**C3.** A strategy wins 38% of the time with an average win of 2.8R. Calculate expectancy.
Then recompute with costs of 0.09R per trade.

**C4.** Calculate the break-even win rate for a reward-to-risk ratio of 1.8.

**C5.** Your account falls from a peak of $22,000 to $15,400. Calculate the drawdown and
the gain required to recover.

**C6.** You risk 3% per trade and lose nine consecutive trades. What percentage of your
account remains, and what gain returns you to break-even?

**C7.** You hold long EUR/USD, short USD/CHF, long AUD/USD and long US 500, each sized at
1% risk. Group these by risk factor and state your true exposure to a strong-dollar
risk-off move.

---

## Part D — Execution and analysis (Modules 05, 07)

**D1.** EUR/USD is at 1.0890. State the order type and price for: entering long only if
price falls to 1.0855; entering short only if price falls below 1.0840.

**D2.** You are long with a stop at 1.0800. The mid price is 1.08055 and the spread widens
from 1.2 pips to 12 pips. Is your stop triggered? Show the bid.

**D3.** Explain why stop losses experience more slippage than take profit orders.

**D4.** ATR(14) on your instrument is 85 pips. Using a 2 ATR stop and 1% risk on a $6,000
account, calculate the position size in lots.

**D5.** Define an uptrend in terms of swing structure, and state what event ends it by
definition.

**D6.** A central bank cuts rates by 0.5% and the currency rises. Give the most likely
explanation.

---

## Part E — Judgement (Modules 02, 08, 09, 10)

**E1.** Define a CFD in one sentence, naming the counterparty and stating what you own.

**E2.** A broker offers you professional client status with 100:1 leverage. Name three
protections you would lose and state your answer.

**E3.** Your backtest shows +1.8R expectancy with an 85% win rate over 120 trades. What is
the most likely explanation, and what do you do?

**E4.** Your forward test shows positive expectancy but 74% plan compliance. What do you
conclude, and what is the correct next step?

**E5.** You have taken four losses today, all within your rules. You notice a fifth setup.
Your plan's daily limit is three losses. What do you do, and what does the fact that you
are asking tell you?

**E6.** A firm regulated in St Vincent and the Grenadines offers 400:1 leverage, a 100%
deposit bonus, and an account manager who will suggest trades. List every red flag and
state what each one indicates.

**E7.** Explain, in terms a non-trader would understand, why a guaranteed 8% monthly return
is not credible.

**E8.** State the single most important number in this course and explain its significance.
