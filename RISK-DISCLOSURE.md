# Risk Disclosure

**Read this before any other file in this repository.**

## What CFDs are, in one sentence

A Contract for Difference (CFD) is a leveraged derivative: you agree with a broker to
exchange the difference in an asset's price between the moment you open a position and
the moment you close it, without ever owning the asset.

## The headline statistic

Regulated CFD brokers in the UK, EU and Australia are legally required to publish the
percentage of their retail clients who lose money. Across firms, that figure has
consistently sat in the **70% to 85%** range, year after year, in rising and falling
markets alike.

That number is not a marketing exaggeration or a scare tactic. It is an audited,
regulator-mandated disclosure. It is the single most important fact in this course.

Read it carefully: it does not say most traders have a bad year. It says that in any
given year, roughly three out of four people doing exactly what this course describes
end that year with less money than they started with.

## What that means for you

- **The default outcome of CFD trading is a loss.** Profitability is the exception, not
  the baseline you fall back to if you try reasonably hard.
- **Leverage magnifies losses exactly as much as it magnifies gains.** At 30:1, a 3.3%
  move against you erases 100% of the margin backing that position.
- **You can lose money faster than you can react.** Markets gap over weekends, around
  economic releases, and during breaking news. A stop loss is not a guarantee.
- **Costs run continuously.** Spread, commission and overnight financing are charged
  whether you are right or wrong. A break-even strategy before costs is a losing
  strategy after them.
- **Time is a real cost too.** Learning this properly takes months. That time has value
  even when the account balance does not move.

## Jurisdiction

CFDs are **not available to retail clients in the United States**. The CFTC and SEC do
not permit them. If you are a US resident, this course is useful as conceptual
education about leverage, margin and risk, but you cannot legally open a retail CFD
account with a regulated broker.

CFDs are available to retail clients in the UK, the EU/EEA, Australia, Singapore, South
Africa, the UAE and many other jurisdictions, each with its own rules on leverage caps,
margin close-out and client money protection. **Verify the rules where you actually
live before doing anything.** Module 10 covers how.

## What this course is not

- It is **not financial advice**. Nothing here is a recommendation to buy, sell or hold
  any instrument, or to open an account with any broker.
- It is **not tax advice**. Tax treatment of CFDs varies enormously by country and by
  your personal circumstances.
- It is **not a promise of profit.** No course, strategy, indicator, signal service or
  mentor can deliver that, and anyone claiming otherwise is telling you something false.
- It is **not a shortcut**. If you are looking for a quick way to make money, close this
  repository. That is the honest answer, and it is the one that costs you the least.

## What this course is

An accurate, practical explanation of how CFD trading actually works: the contract, the
arithmetic, the costs, the risk controls, and the decisions you have to make. It is
written so that if you decide **not** to trade CFDs after finishing it, you will have
made that decision for good reasons. That is a legitimate and common outcome, and a
successful one.

## Rules of engagement for this course

1. Trade on a **demo account** for the entire duration of the course. No exceptions.
2. Do not fund a live account until you have completed the capstone in Module 11 and
   have a written trading plan.
3. If you do go live, start with money whose total loss would change nothing material
   about your life.
4. Never trade with borrowed money, credit, or funds earmarked for rent, debt, tuition,
   healthcare or anyone who depends on you.

## If trading stops being a decision

Leveraged trading shares mechanics with gambling: fast feedback, variable rewards, and
the ability to double down. If you notice yourself chasing losses, hiding trading from
people close to you, trading to change how you feel, or trading money you told yourself
you would not, stop and seek support.

- **UK:** GamCare, 0808 8020 133, https://www.gamcare.org.uk
- **EU:** national helplines vary; see https://www.begambleaware.org for signposting
- **Australia:** Gambling Help Online, 1800 858 858, https://www.gamblinghelponline.org.au
- **US:** 1-800-GAMBLER, https://www.ncpgambling.org
- **International directory:** https://www.gamblingtherapy.org

There is no shame in this and it is more common than the industry admits.
